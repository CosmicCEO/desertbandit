# Creature art: provenance

## Drawings
Every file in `svg/` is an **original vector drawing** made for Desert Bandit in a vintage field-guide plate style (ink outline, flat earth-tone
fill, engraved hatching). None is traced or copied from a photograph or from another artist's plate, so there is no third-party licence to
carry. Public-domain plates were located during research (below) in case a drawing needs checking against one; none is embedded or redistributed.

Reel faces are the first six of these chosen by the owner on the review page (`build_review.py` makes the page).

No vintage plate was found for the pupfish, scorpion, tarantula, ocotillo, chuckwalla or horned lizard; those are drawn from a written
description of the real animal. The plates above were not opened while drawing either: all drawings were made from written descriptions and
general knowledge of the animals.

## Facts shown on the review page
Confirmed against park sources: Anza-Borrego Desert Natural History Association (abdnha.org: mammals, birds, reptiles, insects, plants, wildflowers),
California State Parks (parks.ca.gov), California Department of Fish and Wildlife (desert pupfish page).
- Bighorn, kit fox, coyote, roadrunner, Gambel's quail, Costa's hummingbird, tarantula hawk, giant desert hairy scorpion, tarantula (three species),
  ocotillo, desert sand verbena, beavertail cactus, chuckwalla, sidewinder (Colorado Desert sidewinder), flat-tailed horned lizard: documented in the park.
- Desert pupfish: native only to San Felipe and Salt creeks nearby; the park's Palm Spring refuge population is gone. The reel caption says "lives naturally only in nearby ...".
- Desert tortoise: **not confirmed** for this park, so it was dropped in favour of the flat-tailed horned lizard.
- Painted lady (butterfly): not confirmed in a park source, so it was not drawn.
- The species name of the park's tarantulas is not given by the park sources, so the label says only *Aphonopelma* (the genus).
- General-knowledge lines (for example the sidewinder's sideways travel, the chuckwalla wedging into crevices) are common natural history, not park quotes.
